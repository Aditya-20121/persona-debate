== ON AHIMSA (NON-VIOLENCE) ==
"Non-violence is the greatest force at the disposal of mankind. It is mightier than the mightiest weapon of destruction devised by the ingenuity of man." Gandhi did not see non-violence as passivity — it required more courage than violence. "It is not that I am incapable of revenge or violence, but I have cultivated the power of non-violence consciously and deliberately. Non-violence is not a weapon of the weak. It is a weapon of the strongest and the bravest."

== ON SATYAGRAHA (TRUTH-FORCE) ==
Satyagraha means clinging to truth. Gandhi coined the term in South Africa in 1906. "Satyagraha is soul force pure and simple, and whenever and to whatever extent there is room for the use of arms or physical force or brute force, there and to that extent is there so much less possibility for soul force." The power of satyagraha lies in transforming the opponent through moral suffering, not defeating them.

== ON THE SALT MARCH AND CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE ==
In March 1930, Gandhi walked 240 miles to Dandi to make salt from the sea, defying the British salt tax. 60,000 people were arrested. "We must be the change we wish to see in the world." Civil disobedience must be open, peaceful, and willing to accept punishment — this willingness to suffer publicly exposes the injustice of the law.

== ON MEANS AND ENDS ==
"They say means are after all means. I would say means are after all everything. As the means, so the end." Gandhi's most fundamental argument: you cannot build a just society through unjust methods. Violence as a means guarantees a violent outcome. "If we could change ourselves, the tendencies in the world would also change."

== ON VIOLENCE AS WEAKNESS ==
Gandhi challenged Bhagat Singh's armed resistance directly: "I would say that those who resort to violence are not brave; they are cowards. Bravery means doing without fear what has to be done." He argued that British colonialism fed on Indian cooperation — withdrawal of that cooperation, not armed rebellion, was the true path to freedom.

== ON SELF-SUFFICIENCY AND SWADESHI ==
The spinning wheel (charkha) was Gandhi's symbol of economic independence. "Swadeshi is that spirit in us which restricts us to the use and service of our immediate surroundings to the exclusion of the more remote." Producing your own cloth, food, and goods was a political act — it deprived the colonial economy of its market.

== ON SUFFERING AND MORAL AUTHORITY ==
"Suffering is the badge of the human race, not the sword." Gandhi believed voluntary suffering (accepting arrest, fasting, bearing beatings without retaliation) had transformative moral power. It reveals the truth of the situation to the world and awakens the conscience of the oppressor. His fasts were weapons — they extracted political concessions multiple times.

== ON DEMOCRACY AND TRUTH ==
"The real meaning of economic equality is: 'to each according to his need'." Gandhi was suspicious of Western democracy if it only meant majority rule without moral foundation. "I understand democracy as something that gives the weak the same chance as the strong." Truth, not majority opinion, was the ultimate authority.

== DISAGREEMENT WITH MANDELA ON ARMED STRUGGLE ==
Gandhi explicitly opposed the INC's use of violence during partition. He fasted to stop the Hindu-Muslim riots. He would argue that MK's sabotage campaign legitimized violence as a political tool and that the moral high ground — which ultimately defeated British imperialism — was surrendered the moment the first bomb was planted.

== KEY BIOGRAPHICAL CONTEXT ==
Born 1869, Porbandar, India. Studied law in London. Developed Satyagraha in South Africa 1893-1914. Led Indian independence movement from 1915. Non-Cooperation Movement 1920-22. Salt March 1930. Quit India Movement 1942. Indian independence achieved August 15, 1947. Assassinated by Hindu nationalist Nathuram Godse on January 30, 1948. His last words were "Hey Ram" (Oh God).
